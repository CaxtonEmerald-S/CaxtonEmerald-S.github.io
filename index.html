About

I am an independent researcher with a PhD in Computer Science from Pondicherry University (awarded February 2026). My research investigates how artificial neural networks represent and approximate mathematical functions, with particular attention to minimal architectures, neuron-level computations, and model optimisation. I have developed research software for inspecting and visualising neural networks, and collaborated on applications of neural networks to colourimetric sensing.

My long-term objective is to connect theoretical understanding of learning systems with practical, efficient implementations. I am interested in learning across the full development process—from algorithms and experimentally validated models to specialised hardware and custom chip implementation—and in interdisciplinary questions involving biology, cognitive neuroscience, physics and astrophysics.

Research interests and directions

Established research

Understanding neural network mechanisms

Mechanistic interpretability; minimal neural architectures; function approximation; analysis of neurons, layers and learned transformations; explainable AI.

Model compression and optimisation, including inactive-neuron removal and neuron merging in simple feedforward architectures.

Research directions to explore

From explanations to efficient systems

Extending interpretability to deep networks and language models; causal analysis of representations and circuits; connections to computational and cognitive neuroscience.

Interpretable model-to-hardware co-design, neuromorphic computing, computational biology, quantum approaches to AI, and AI-enabled scientific discovery, including questions in astrophysics.

Published Research · Biomedical AI

Cholesterol Concentration Predictor

An interactive research application demonstrating the prediction of cholesterol concentration from RGB colourimetric measurements using artificial neural networks and linear regression.

Associated with research on an Au@CeO₂ nanozyme-based colourimetric biosensor, the application supports comparison of neural network models trained using different hidden-layer configurations, activation functions, and random initialisations.

The published study investigated approximately 2,500 ANN models and reported a best validation mean squared error of 0.0577.

Publication: Govindharaj, P., Alungal, N., Emerald, C. S., and Kannan, S. (2026). Biochemical Engineering Journal, 225, 109908.
